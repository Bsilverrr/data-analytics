5. Why did the line for Milkshake change? Hint: Click on the icon next to “Interest
over time” for more information about how trend results are displayed.

Google Trends normalizes everything to a 0–100 scale, and 100 is the single highest point across every term in the comparison. Alone, Milkshake’s own peak was 100. Once you add Hamburger, Hamburger’s peak becomes the new 100 and Milkshake gets rescaled down against it. The data didn’t change, the scale did.

7. What does all of this suggest about the reliability of the conclusions reached in the
article?

In my opinion id say weak because search interest measures curiosity, not behavior. Nothing connects a milkshake search to an actual breakup. The values are relative, so “milkshakes win in six states” only means milkshake outranked the other foods on that list in those states. The food list came from three blog posts, not from any survey of what people actually eat. The term “Milkshake” captures recipes, fast food menus, and more. Which is no way to isolate breakup related searches.
