Exercise 3.A Reviewing Survey Results

For each survey question:
1. What conclusion can you reach about the responses to this question?
2. Is the default summary or visualization helpful? How could it be improved?
3. For questions with an "Other" option, where can you find the actual text of those responses? Do you notice anything that might be tricky about analyzing these?
4. For questions calling for a written response, what challenges do you notice about finding trends or patterns in the answers provided?
5. Now that you can see the responses, how might you recommend rephrasing this question or the response options in a future survey?


QUESTION 1: What do you envision yourself doing 5 minutes before
class starts each day, to ensure that you are ready to start on time?

1. Most people said the same few things: checking that their tech
works, joining the Zoom link early, and reviewing notes from the last
class.

2. The word cloud is okay but the biggest term is "minutes before
class," which is just the question repeated back. Half the class
echoed the wording, so the chart highlights noise instead of actual
answers.

3. N/A, this one is free text.

4. The word cloud counts single words, so it can't tell that "review
my notes" and "look over material" mean the same thing. Everyone
words it differently and some answers cover more than one topic.

5. Turn it into checkboxes for the common prep steps with an Other
field, so the answers can actually be counted.

QUESTION 2: Do you have a second monitor connected to your computer?

1. The chart says No 13, Yes 2, Other 9. But reading the Other
answers, 8 of them say "Yes, I have second monitor." So the real
count is 10 Yes out of 24, about 42%, not 8%.

2. No. The donut chart is actually wrong. If you only looked at the
chart you would think almost nobody has a second monitor, when it is
closer to half the class.

3. Click More details to see the Other text. The tricky part is that
almost all of the Other answers are Yes answers that got typed out
instead of selected, so they never get counted as Yes. There is also
one "Maybe," which is not a valid answer to a yes/no question.

4. Same issue, the written answers all mean the same thing but none
of them match the Yes option exactly.

5. Keep it Yes/No but define what counts, like whether a tablet or a
second laptop counts as a second monitor.


QUESTION 3: Do you have an external keyboard and mouse?


1. Same thing as question 2. Chart says No 13, Yes 2, Other 9, but 6
of the Other answers say "Yes, I have an external mouse and
keyboard." Real total is 8 Yes out of 24.

2. No, for the same reason. The chart undercounts Yes by a lot.

3. More details. Two answers were "External mouse, not
keyboard" and "I have a mouse," which shows the question is asking
about two things at once. People cannot answer Yes or No
honestly.

4. The written answers are all slightly different versions of yes.

5. Split it into two questions, or use checkboxes for keyboard /
mouse / neither.


QUESTION 4: How do you plan to take notes for this class - in a doc
on your computer, with pen and paper, or something else?


1. The chart says "With pen and paper 1, Other 23," which is useless.
Reading the actual answers, it is pen and paper 10, computer 8, both
5, iPad 1.

2. No. Only one person's answer matched the one option exactly, so
96% went into Other. The chart shows nothing real.

3. More details. A few problems I noticed:
   - "With pen and paper" and "With pen and paper. I have a
     designated notebook." are the same answer but counted
     separately, which splits 10 people into 9 and 1.
   - One answer came through as ["On my computer"] with brackets and
     quotes, so formatting characters leaked into the data.
   - 4 people answered "A and B," but the form has no lettered
     options, so the question must have changed after people
     responded.

4. Nothing groups on its own. You have to read all 24 and sort them
by hand.

5. Add the options the question actually names: on my computer, pen
and paper, tablet, and both.


QUESTION 5: What is one question that you have right now, about this
course generally or about a specific topic we'll be talking about?

1. People mostly want to know what tools they will learn and how it
applies to a real job. The word cloud shows data, Data Analyst, AI,
python, predictive analytics, skills, and languages.

2. The word cloud is okay but "data" is the biggest word, which tells
you nothing in a data analytics class. The smaller terms around it
are where the useful stuff is.

3. N/A

4. At least one answer was "So far, I have no questions," which is
not a question and would have to be filtered out before analyzing.

5. Add a "No questions right now" checkbox so you can tell the
difference between people who skipped it and people who had nothing
to ask.


6. Is there anything that surprised you about the survey responses?


How wrong the charts were. All three multiple choice questions had
huge Other buckets full of answers that should have been counted as
Yes or as one of the listed options. If I had only looked at the
summary page I would have gotten every single one of them wrong.


7. Looking at the responses to questions 1 and 5, are there any ideas
you might try to include in your morning routine, or any questions
that you are also curious about?


Joining the Zoom early and reviewing the last class's notes in those
five minutes instead of just logging in at the exact time without doing so. Questions I also have:
how much SQL and Python we actually cover, and how this applies to
real analyst work.


8. Pretend you are the instructor. Is there anything you can learn
from this survey to help in how you approach teaching this class?


The equipment answers are worth checking by hand before assuming
anything, since the charts are off. Beyond that, people clearly want
to know what tools they will learn and how the work translates to an
actual job, so setting those expectations early would help.

9. Again, pretend you are the instructor. Do you think it was helpful
or not helpful to make this survey anonymous? Why or why not?


It was anonymous, and I think that was the right call. People
admitted stuff like drinking coffee and waking up and asked how
hard the course would get, which they might not say with their name
on it. The tradeoff is the instructor cannot follow up with the
people who asked about tutoring, because there is no way to know who
they are.
