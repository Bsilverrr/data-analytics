1. Start by reviewing the data.gov home page. What is this?

It’s the US Government open data site, described as “the home of the U.S. Government’s open data.” It’s a catalog of datasets published by federal, state, and local agencies.

2. Go to Most Viewed Datasets. How many datasets are listed?

583,997 

3. Find the Topic Category for Human Health. Review the range of data sources
available for this topic. Why do you think these were tagged with the Human Health
category? 

The datasets tagged by US Department of Health cover things like healthcare quality, environmental exposure, nutrition, and disease surveillance. These different sets are grouped together because each one has to deal with something that affects human health.

4. Go to the Food Price Outlook. Review the catalog page for this dataset. How can you
navigate to additional datasets from the USDA?

The catalog page lists the publisher as the USDA Economic Research Service. Clicking the organization name pulls up every dataset from that agency(Department of Agriculture).

b) Go back to the home page. Find Foundation Foods, and click on Downloads. What
kind of data can you find on this page? How big are these files? Where can you find
more information on the data types available

The Downloads page offers Foundation Foods as CSV files, released twice a year. The files range from about 210K to 1.8M, and unzipped from 291K to 25M. More information about the data types is on the data type documentation page, which explains what each dataset contains and how often it’s updated.
